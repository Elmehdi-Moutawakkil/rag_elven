The Valar: The Powers of Arda

The Valar are the great Ainur who descended into Arda (the world) at the beginning of time to shape and govern it according to the vision of Iluvatar. The name "Valar" means "the Powers" in Quenya. They are not gods in the absolute sense, but rather angelic beings of immense power who serve Iluvatar and act as stewards of creation.

There are fourteen Valar in total: seven male (called Valar) and seven female (called Valier). The greatest among them is Manwe, Lord of the Winds and King of Arda, who sits upon his throne on Mount Taniquetil, the highest peak in the world. His consort is Varda, Lady of the Stars (called Elbereth by the Elves), who is loved above all Valar by the Elves.

The principal Valar are:

Manwe — Lord of the Winds and King of Arda. Highest of all Valar in authority. He hears the thoughts of all who dwell in Arda. His eagles are his messengers.

Varda (Elbereth) — Lady of the Stars. She kindled the stars of heaven and set the constellations. The Elves love and revere her above all others; her name is a cry against darkness.

Ulmo — Lord of Waters. He dwells not in Valinor but in the deep waters of the world. He never lost interest in the fate of the Children of Iluvatar and often sent warnings through rivers and seas.

Aule — Lord of the Earth and Maker. Master of craft and material skill. He created the substance of Arda. Out of impatience and love, he secretly fashioned the Dwarves before the awakening of the Elves.

Yavanna — Giver of Fruits, wife of Aule. She created all growing things. She made the Two Trees of Valinor and fashioned the Ents as guardians of forests.

Namo (Mandos) — Keeper of the Dead. His halls in Valinor receive the spirits of fallen Elves. He speaks doom and judgement, and his pronouncements are seldom wrong.

Nienna — Lady of Mercy and Grief. She weeps for the suffering of the world. Gandalf (Olorin) was among her students before coming to Middle-earth.

Orome — Lord of Forests and Huntsman of the Valar. He rode through Middle-earth during the dark years and was the first Vala to discover the Elves at Cuivienen.

Tulkas — Champion of the Valar. He has no weapon, for his strength is in his hands. He was the last to descend into Arda, arriving to help defeat Melkor in the first great war.

Melkor and His Fall: The Origin of Morgoth

Melkor was the greatest of all the Ainur created by Iluvatar. He was given the greatest gifts of power and knowledge, and he had a share of all the abilities of the other Valar. Yet his greatness became his downfall.

Before the world was made, Iluvatar revealed to the Ainur the great Music (the Ainulindale) through which the world would be created. Melkor wove into this Music themes of his own devising, seeking power and glory for himself. This discord produced dissonance and suffering in the Music, but Iluvatar overcame every discordant theme and wove it into the design.

When Arda was first formed, Melkor claimed dominion over it. He raised great mountains, dug deep pits, and lit enormous fires — but the other Valar built up what he destroyed, and he broke down what they built. In the first wars of the Valar, Melkor was eventually driven out of Arda.

The Two Trees and the First Great Betrayal

After the Valar established Valinor and created the Two Trees (Telperion the Silver Tree and Laurelin the Golden Tree, whose light illuminated the Blessed Realm), there was a period of peace. The Elves were invited to dwell in Valinor, and many came — the Vanyar, the Noldor, and part of the Teleri.

Melkor was eventually captured, brought before the Valar, and imprisoned in the Halls of Mandos for three ages. When released, he feigned repentance and was permitted to dwell in Valinor. There he moved among the Elves, particularly the Noldor, spreading lies and discontent.

The greatest of his lies was to tell the Noldor that the Valar had brought them to Valinor as prisoners, keeping them from their rightful dominion over Middle-earth. He whispered that the Silmarils — the three jewels of Feanor containing the light of the Two Trees — were coveted by Manwe himself.

The Theft of the Silmarils and the Darkening of Valinor

Feanor, the greatest craftsman among the Elves, had forged the Silmarils — three jewels of incomparable beauty that captured the living light of the Two Trees. Melkor desired them above all things.

Melkor fled Valinor and allied himself with Ungoliant, a great spirit of darkness in the form of a monstrous spider. Together they attacked the Two Trees: Ungoliant devoured their light while Melkor struck them with his black spear, poisoning the roots. The Trees died, and Valinor was plunged into darkness.

Melkor and Ungoliant then attacked the vault of Feanor's father Finwe, killed him (the first death of an Elf by violence), and stole the Silmarils. This was the Darkening of Valinor, the greatest catastrophe before the end of the world.

After this act, Melkor was no longer called by his original name. Feanor cursed him with the name Morgoth, meaning "Black Enemy" in Sindarin (Quenya: Moringotto). This name he bore until the end of his days.

Morgoth in Middle-earth: The First Age

Morgoth fled to Middle-earth and established his fortress of Angband in the far northwest, beneath the Iron Mountains (Ered Engrin). From Angband he bred and corrupted countless creatures: Orcs (corrupted Elves, according to some accounts), Balrogs (Maiar of fire corrupted by Morgoth), Dragons (the first of whom was Glaurung), and other monsters.

His greatest fortress was Utumno, built in the ancient days, but after its destruction by the Valar, he rebuilt Angband as his seat of power in the First Age.

The Three Great Peaks of Thangorodrim — three volcanic mountains — rose above Angband like a crown of smoke and fire.

The Silmarillion of the First Age is largely the story of the Noldor Elves who came back from Valinor to Middle-earth to wage war on Morgoth and recover the Silmarils. This war lasted for nearly six hundred years and is known as the Wars of Beleriand.

Despite several great victories by the Elves and their allies — including the Union of Maedhros and the various heroic deeds like Beren and Luthien stealing a Silmaril from Morgoth's crown — the war ultimately ended in catastrophe for the Elves.

The War of Wrath and the Fall of Morgoth

At the end of the First Age, Earendil the Mariner sailed to Valinor bearing a Silmaril and begged the Valar to intervene against Morgoth. His prayer was granted. The Valar sent an overwhelming host — the War of Wrath — that broke Angband utterly.

Morgoth was thrown down, his iron crown beaten into a collar, and the two remaining Silmarils taken. He was cast through the Door of Night beyond the Walls of the World into the Void, where he is imprisoned until the Last Battle at the end of time (the Dagor Dagorath).

Yet the damage was done. Beleriand was largely destroyed and sank beneath the sea at the end of the War of Wrath. The evil Morgoth had bred — Orcs, Trolls, Dragons, Werewolves — survived his fall and scattered across the world.

---

The Legacy of Morgoth

The concept of Morgoth's corrupting influence on Arda is central to Tolkien's mythology. Tolkien wrote of what he called the "Morgoth's Ring" concept: that Melkor poured so much of his power and will into the physical substance of Arda itself that the world was permanently tainted. Evil is literally woven into the fabric of Middle-earth.

This is why good can never be complete in the world until the Last Battle: Morgoth's corrupting presence remains in the earth, in fire, in cold, in disease — not as a conscious will, but as a residual corruption that generates monsters, plagues, and evil impulses.

Sauron, who served as Morgoth's lieutenant in the First Age, continued his master's work in the Second and Third Ages, though on a smaller scale — for Sauron never achieved the universal domination that Morgoth had briefly held.

The Elves who survived the First Age and came to Middle-earth carried with them the memory of Valinor before the Darkening: the light of the Two Trees, preserved only in the Silmarils (of which one is now the Evening Star carried by Earendil), and in the hearts of the Eldar.
