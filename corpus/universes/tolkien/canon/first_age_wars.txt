Beleriand and the Wars of the First Age

Beleriand was the great land in the northwest of Middle-earth during the First Age. It was bounded by the sea to the west, the mountains of Ered Luin to the east, the highlands of Dorthonion to the north, and the sea again to the south. This land was the setting for almost all the great events of the First Age. At the end of the First Age, most of Beleriand was destroyed and sank beneath the sea during the War of Wrath.

The kingdoms of Beleriand were primarily Elvish, home to both the Sindar (Grey Elves who had never gone to Valinor) and the returning Noldor (High Elves who came back from Valinor to war on Morgoth). The greatest human tribes, known as the Edain, also entered Beleriand during this period and became allies of the Elves.

---

The Awakening and the Siege of Angband

When Morgoth fled from Valinor with the stolen Silmarils, he returned to his ancient fortress of Angband in the far northwest of Middle-earth. There he built Thangorodrim — three great volcanic peaks — over the gates of Angband and began to breed armies of Orcs, Trolls, and Balrogs.

The Noldor returned to Middle-earth shortly after, led by Feanor and his sons, swearing the Oath of Feanor to recover the Silmarils at any cost. Their arrival in Beleriand was marked by two great crimes: the Kinslaying at Alqualonde (where they killed other Elves to steal their ships) and the burning of those ships at Losgar, abandoning those who had not yet crossed.

After Feanor's death in the Dagor-nuin-Giliath (the Battle Under Stars), his sons continued the war. For nearly four centuries, the Noldor maintained the Siege of Angband — surrounding Morgoth's fortress and preventing him from breaking out. During this time, peace largely held in Beleriand.

---

The Five Great Battles of Beleriand

The history of the First Age is organized around five major battles that marked the turning points of the war against Morgoth.

The First Battle — Dagor-nuin-Giliath (The Battle Under Stars)
This was the first battle after the Noldor's return to Middle-earth. Feanor himself led the assault but was mortally wounded by Gothmog, High Captain of Balrogs. His sons continued the battle and won a significant victory, driving Morgoth's forces back to Angband. However, the loss of Feanor — the greatest craftsman and perhaps the most powerful of the Noldor — was a devastating blow.

The Second Battle — Dagor Aglareb (The Glorious Battle)
A major assault by Morgoth's forces was crushed by the combined armies of the Noldor. The victory was so complete that the Noldor established the Siege of Angband, surrounding Morgoth's fortress for four centuries. It seemed that Morgoth might eventually be defeated. But Morgoth used this time to breed ever larger armies in secret beneath the mountains.

The Third Battle — Dagor Bragollach (The Battle of Sudden Flame)
After nearly four hundred years of siege, Morgoth unleashed rivers of fire from Thangorodrim that burned the plains of Ard-galen to ash (it was renamed Anfauglith, the Gasping Dust). This catastrophic assault broke the Siege of Angband permanently.

The Dagor Bragollach was one of the worst defeats of the Noldor. Fingolfin, High King of the Noldor, rode alone to the gates of Angband in despair and grief, challenged Morgoth to single combat, and wounded him seven times before being slain. Morgoth limped ever after from those wounds and he alone among the Valar knew the pain of physical injury.

Many Noldor lords were slain, their kingdoms shattered. Only scattered strongholds held out — Gondolin (hidden), Nargothrond (hidden), and Doriath (protected by Melian's Girdle).

The Fourth Battle — Nirnaeth Arnoediad (The Battle of Unnumbered Tears)
Also called the Union of Maedhros, this was a massive coordinated assault by all remaining Elvish kingdoms plus their human allies, intended to crush Morgoth once and for all.

It nearly succeeded. But treachery among the Easterlings (men allied with Morgoth) opened gaps in the line at a critical moment. The battle turned into a catastrophic rout. Fingon, High King of the Noldor, was slain. Hurin of Dor-lomin was captured and placed on a mountain to watch the tragedies that would befall his family. Glorfindel, Gothmog, and countless lords fell on both sides.

The Nirnaeth Arnoediad effectively ended organized Elvish resistance to Morgoth. After it, the remaining free kingdoms — Gondolin, Nargothrond, and Doriath — stood isolated and eventually fell one by one.

The Fall of Nargothrond, Doriath, and Gondolin followed in the subsequent decades.

The Fifth Battle — the War of Wrath (Dagor Dagorath Veliceva, the Great Battle)
Technically not a single battle but a war, the War of Wrath was the Valar's final intervention. Earendil the Mariner, bearing the last Silmaril, sailed to Valinor and begged the Valar to save the peoples of Middle-earth. His prayer was answered.

The host of Valinor — Valar, Maiar, and armies of Elves — descended on Middle-earth. Morgoth's forces were utterly destroyed. His Balrogs were almost entirely wiped out (Durin's Bane in Moria was one of the last survivors). Ancalagon the Black, the greatest of all Dragons, was slain by Earendil in aerial combat. Morgoth himself was thrown down, stripped of power, and cast through the Door of Night into the Void.

Beleriand was so devastated by the War of Wrath that most of it sank beneath the sea, forming the Gulf of Lune in the northwest of what became known as Middle-earth proper.

Key Heroes of the First Age

Feanor (Curufinwe Feanaro) — Greatest craftsman of the Noldor, maker of the Silmarils and the Palantiri. His Oath drove the Noldor back to Middle-earth but also caused untold suffering. He burned with such inner fire that his body consumed itself — his funeral pyre was said to be the brightest fire ever seen.

Fingolfin — High King of the Noldor, brother of Feanor. His single combat with Morgoth is one of the most celebrated acts of defiance in Tolkien's legendarium. He wounded the Dark Lord seven times before falling.

Maedhros and Maglor — Sons of Feanor. Maedhros was captured by Morgoth and hung from a cliff by one wrist for years before being rescued by Fingon. He became one of the great leaders of the Noldor despite losing his right hand. Maglor was the greatest singer of the Noldor.

Beren and Luthien — The greatest love story of the First Age. Beren was a mortal Man; Luthien was the daughter of Thingol and the Maia Melian, the most beautiful of all Elves. Together they penetrated Angband, cast Morgoth into sleep, and cut a Silmaril from his crown. Luthien is the only being said to have moved the heart of Mandos: she sang before the Vala and won a second life for herself and Beren.

Turin Turambar — Son of Hurin, cursed by Morgoth to a life of tragedy. He slew the dragon Glaurung with the black sword Gurthang (Anglachel), but the dragon's dying curse revealed truths that drove Turin to suicide. His story is one of the most tragic in Tolkien's legendarium.

Earendil — Son of Tuor (Man) and Idril (Elf of Gondolin). He married Elwing, granddaughter of Beren and Luthien, and together they bore a Silmaril. He sailed to Valinor and his plea brought the intervention of the Valar. He was set in the sky as a star bearing the Silmaril — the Evening Star (Venus) — as a sign of hope for the people of Middle-earth.

Glorfindel — A lord of the House of the Golden Flower in Gondolin. He slew a Balrog during the Fall of Gondolin, giving his life to protect the fugitives. He was later re-embodied in Valinor and sent back to Middle-earth in the Third Age as an emissary of the Valar.

---

The Legacy of the First Age

The First Age established the mythological foundation of all later Tolkien history. The Silmarils that were fought over for centuries ended thus: one was taken into the sky (Earendil's star), one sank into the sea (thrown in by Maedhros in grief), and one plunged into the earth (thrown in by Maglor in grief). The Oath of Feanor was thus fulfilled, but at incalculable cost.

The surviving Elves of the First Age who came to Middle-earth carried a profound grief: the memory of Beleriand and all they had lost. This underlying sadness — the sense of inevitable fading and loss — runs through all Elvish culture in the Second and Third Ages.

The Edain (Men who fought alongside the Elves) were rewarded after the War of Wrath with the island of Numenor, set in the Great Sea between Middle-earth and Valinor. Their descendants became the Numenoreans and, later, the Men of Gondor and Arnor.
